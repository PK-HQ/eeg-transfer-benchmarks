# Frozen clinical cohort design

Use all eight healthy and ten baseline stroke recordings from NEMAR v1.0.1. Source identities and source cue/go marker pairs must agree with provenance and BIDS event labels and samples. Carry forward the pilot's chronological split at the trial midpoint, dropping the midpoint trial as a guard. Use the earliest 2, 5, 10, or 20 trials per class from the first half. Evaluate every model on the same later block. No parameter selection uses patient data. The pilot informed the design and its four participants are included for cohort completeness.

Use the pilot's trial-local features, subject/class-balanced source fitting, and fixed L2 penalty of 10. Compare 15 motor electrodes to 15 non-motor electrodes with identical bands and regularization. The non-motor set comprises FP1, FPZ, FP2, AF3, AF4, P3, PZ, P4, PO3, POZ, PO4, O1, OZ, O2, and PO8. Whole-scalp models have more features and serve as a separate benchmark. Compare early visual, ocular, and pre-cue features and compare combined visual/ocular features with and without motor power. Incremental prediction can still reflect cue-related activity.

Use raw features as the reference and a label-independent robust sensitivity analysis. Clip each feature window's channel trace to ±150 µV around its own median before feature extraction, including bipolar EOG after differencing. Retain every trial and freeze the test set across the two analyses. Count late-window scalp ranges above 300 µV or below 1 µV and bipolar EOG ranges above 300 µV. These flags describe potential artifacts and saturation/flatness, with no attribution to a specific source. Clipping limits the influence of excursions and can also attenuate physiological signal.

Report balanced accuracy, class recalls and fist prediction fractions per patient. Use paired percentile bootstrap intervals resampling patients, 10,000 replicates with seed 2026. These are descriptive intervals conditional on the healthy training cohort. Treat calibration budgets and controls as descriptive comparisons. Evaluate both a healthy coefficient prior and a zero coefficient prior in the same healthy-fitted feature space.
